Travel Guide — Arabian Sea

Lakshadweep, an island world you plan for and never forget.

● 10 min read ● Best window: Oct – May ● 36 islands, few open to visitors

Turquoise lagoons, coral reefs, and a pace of travel that runs on tides and permits rather than timetables. Here's what to know before you book.

Why go

Lakshadweep is one of India's most beautiful tropical destinations, known for its turquoise waters, coral reefs, white-sand beaches, and peaceful island atmosphere in the Arabian Sea. It offers a completely different pace from crowded city breaks — but the trade-off is preparation: permits, transport, and accommodation all work differently here than on the mainland.

The islands suit travelers who want beautiful beaches and lagoons, coral reefs and marine biodiversity, snorkeling and diving, and a genuinely peaceful setting — whether that's a couple's escape, a family holiday, or a slow solo trip.

Where to go

Five islands worth building an itinerary around

Only a handful of Lakshadweep's islands are open to tourists, and each has its own character — from administrative Kavaratti to castaway-quiet Bangaram.

01 — Agatti

Agatti Island

One of the most scenic islands, known for its lagoon and clear blue water — a strong pick if you want scenery paired with a peaceful island pace.

02 — Bangaram

Bangaram Island

Tranquil and tropical, with sandy beaches built for disconnecting. A favorite for couples and anyone chasing a proper relaxing break.

03 — Kavaratti

Kavaratti Island

The administrative capital — beaches, lagoons, local culture, and marine attractions in one place. Good for travelers who want more than sand.

04 — Kalpeni

Kalpeni Island

A coral-rich lagoon and a genuinely peaceful setting, with snorkeling and other water activities depending on conditions on the day.

05 — Kadmat

Kadmat Island

Long sandy beaches and clear water, well suited to water activities and a slower travel pace by the sea.

Which island fits your trip depends on pace, budget, and how much water time you want — a good travel agency can match you to one.

Things to do

An island holiday isn't just the beach

Water is the main event — but there's a version of it for every comfort level, from staying dry to going deep.

🤿

Snorkeling

The easiest way into Lakshadweep's underwater world. With a guided operator, even beginners can enjoy the coral and colorful marine life.

🫧

Scuba diving

For a deeper look at the reefs and ecosystems — always through authorized, professionally operated dive centers.

🛶

Kayaking

A relaxed way to explore the calm waters around the islands, without the intensity of the bigger water sports.

🚤

Glass-bottom boats

See the marine life from a dry seat — a good option for families or anyone not swimming or diving.

🌅

Beach time

Walks, sunsets, and photography by the lagoon. Often the most memorable part of the trip is the simplest.

Planning

When to go, and how to get there

Timing

Best time to visit

October to May is generally the more favorable window for tourism and water activities. During the monsoon, sea conditions and weather can affect transport and outdoor plans — always check the latest conditions before locking in dates, especially if water sports are the priority.

Getting there

By air & by ship

Flights connect to Agatti Airport, the main air gateway, with onward transport depending on your island and package. Passenger ships also run from the mainland, taking longer but offering a different kind of journey. Schedules vary, so confirm before you plan around them.

Permits

Entry requirements

Travel to Lakshadweep needs specific permits, which can depend on nationality, itinerary, and current regulations. Rules change, so verify the latest official requirements — and ask your travel agency whether permit assistance is included in the package.

Duration

How many days

A 4–6 day itinerary is usually enough for a first trip to cover the main attractions without feeling rushed. Add more days if you want to slow down, dive deeper into water activities, or visit multiple islands.

Who it suits

Couples, families, and slow travelers

For couples

Peaceful beaches and scenic surroundings make Lakshadweep a strong pick for couples. A slower itinerary — fewer islands, more time in each — tends to beat trying to see everything.

For families

A great choice for families who enjoy beaches and the outdoors, though it's worth checking transport, medical facilities, and activity requirements before choosing an itinerary. A well-planned package smooths most of this out.

Before you go

Seven things first-time visitors should know

01

Plan early

Accommodation and transport are more limited than on the mainland — book ahead to secure what you want.

02

Sort your permits

Confirm required permits and documents are arranged well before you travel.

03

Keep the itinerary loose

Weather and sea conditions can shift inter-island transport — don't overbook your schedule.

04

Respect the reef

The islands' beauty depends on the marine ecosystem — avoid touching coral, disturbing wildlife, or leaving waste.

05

Follow water-safety guidance

Always follow professional guides during snorkeling, diving, boating, or any water activity.

06

Pack essentials

Smaller islands may not stock everything — bring important personal items with you.

07

Read the package fine print

Check exactly what's included: accommodation, meals, transfers, activities, permits, and transport.

Packing

What to bring

Check with your tour operator about snorkeling or dive equipment before you pack your own — the rest of this list is on you.

  • Lightweight, comfortable clothing
  • Swimwear
  • Sunglasses & sunscreen
  • Hat or cap
  • Comfortable footwear & beach sandals
  • Personal medicines
  • Reusable water bottle
  • Power bank
  • Waterproof phone pouch
  • Small first-aid kit
  • ID & travel documents
  • Confirmed permits
Quick answers

Frequently asked

Is Lakshadweep good for couples?

Yes — peaceful beaches, lagoon sunsets, and water activities suit a slower, romantic itinerary better than trying to cover many islands at once.

Is it suitable for families with children?

Generally yes, though it's worth checking transport options, medical facilities, and activity requirements for your specific islands before booking.

How many days should I plan for?

4–6 days works well for a first visit. Add time if you want to island-hop or spend longer in the water.

Do I need a permit?

Yes — requirements vary by nationality and itinerary and can change, so confirm the latest official rules before you travel, and check whether your travel package includes permit assistance.
